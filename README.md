**About**
This prototype is developed for **Hainanese**, an under-documented variety of Min Chinese spoken primarily on the island of Hainan and among overseas Hainanese communities.
According to Wikipedia:
Hainanese[b] is a variety of Min Chinese spoken on the island of Hainan and by regional overseas Chinese communities.

In the classification by Yuan Jiahua, used by Ethnologue, it is part of the Southern Min group despite being mutually unintelligible with Southern Min varieties such as Hokkien and Teochew.[6] In the classification of Li Rong, used by the Language Atlas of China, it is treated as a separate Min subgroup.[7] Hou Jingyi combines it with Leizhou Min, spoken on the Leizhou Peninsula, in a Qiong–Lei group.

**V0.2.0**

**How It Works**
The prototype uses a developing Hainanese dictionary as its linguistic base, with LLMs assisting in contextual interpretation when direct dictionary matching is insufficient.
Because Hainanese shares substantial vocabulary and written forms with Mandarin Chinese, the system preserves compatible Chinese expressions while replacing documented differences with Hainanese forms. This allows the translator to remain usable even while the dictionary is still limited, with translation quality improving as more Hainanese vocabulary is documented.

**Broader Applications**
The same framework could potentially be adapted to other languages and dialects in the Sinosphere by replacing the Hainanese dictionary and making relatively small changes to the LLM instructions.
It may also be applicable to other low-resource varieties when there is a closely related, better-documented language that can serve as the base language. The system is designed around this relationship rather than requiring a complete standalone dictionary before translation becomes possible.

**Dictionary**
The Hainanese dictionary is still under active development and will be published once it reaches a more comprehensive stage.
